Reading Information in a Racing Game: A UX Study of KartRider

Research Methods in Cognitive Science · 2022

Undergrad Course

KartRider, a Korean racing game marketed under the slogan "Crazy Racing," raises a simple question: is the player's goal to race, to drive — or to read the information shown on screen? Elements like the booster gauge, acceleration indicator, minimap, and rearview mirror suggest that playing the game may, in a sense, be the same act as reading information.

Centering the concept of "reading," the project investigated how information should be arranged and visually presented for better gameplay. Using a User Action Framework analysis, user and user-group modeling, and a set of graphic-display options as independent variables — with usability (time-trial records) and user satisfaction as dependent variables — the study combined quantitative and qualitative methods to analyze cognitive, social, and emotional interaction within the game environment.
